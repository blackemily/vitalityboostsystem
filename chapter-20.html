Safety, Interactions, and the Current Label

A practical review of the formula's reported amounts, overlapping supplements, and why medication review matters.

Use the bottle in front of you

Public descriptions and label transcriptions can change. A September 2026 transcription of the seller's label reports a two-capsule serving and eleven active ingredients, but the current bottle should be checked before relying on those amounts or directions. Compare the Supplement Facts, serving size, other ingredients, warnings, manufacturer details, lot number, and expiration date.

Review overlap with other supplements

The formula includes niacinamide and magnesium, which may also appear in multivitamins, B-complex products, mineral formulas, or other supplements. Add up the amounts from all products. The NIH niacin fact sheet describes an adult upper intake level of 35 mg per day for supplemental niacin; the limit is based on flushing from nicotinic acid and is applied broadly for guidance, while niacinamide has a different side-effect profile. The listed 200 mg niacinamide amount deserves a discussion with a healthcare professional rather than an assumption that more B3 means more energy.

The magnesium amount reported for the formula is 45 mg. The NIH upper limit of 350 mg per day applies to magnesium from supplements and medications, not food. People with kidney problems or those using certain medicines should seek individualized guidance.

Check medication interactions

NCCIH notes that CoQ10 may interact with warfarin, insulin, and some cancer treatments. Magnesium can affect absorption of certain antibiotics and oral bisphosphonates. Curcumin and other plant extracts may also deserve review, particularly when a product includes ingredients intended to change absorption.

Give a pharmacist or clinician a list of all medicines and supplements. Do not stop a prescription or change a dose to accommodate a new supplement.

Practical rule: A product being sold without a prescription does not mean it is suitable for every person or compatible with every medication.
